---

Practice and Rehearsal both need to know how long your own lines normally take to say, so they
can time the countdown before your cue and pace the run correctly. **Teach** and **Fill-in-Teach**
are the two ways to record that timing, line by line, before either run mode can run a scene —
in the app's own on-screen language this step is called "Prepare scene"; "Teach"/"Timers" are the
internal/developer names still used in these help articles.

## Choosing which one

From a scene's "Prepare scene" button (Edit mode's bottom row), pick "Missing lines only" or
"All lines":

- **Teach** walks the *whole scene* in order, playing every line — yours and everyone else's —
  so you experience the scene the way it will eventually run.
- **Fill-in-Teach** shows only *your own* lines, one at a time, each with the line(s) that lead
  into it for context — a faster pass once a scene is already partly taught and you just need to
  fill in a handful of missing timers.

Tapping an untaught scene from Practice or Rehearse offers a one-tap "This scene needs a quick
set-up first — do it now?" shortcut straight into the right one of these (Fill-in-Teach if the
scene's partly taught already, otherwise the full Teach flow) rather than a dead end.

## While it's running

Other characters' lines play automatically, spoken by TTS at your set pacing. When the scene
reaches one of your own lines, a live timer starts and the on-screen prompt reads "Counting the
pause for you to speak your line" — say your line at your own pace, then tap **"Next Line"** to
save that timing and move the scene on. "Pause"/"Resume" let you pause the timer mid-line if you
need a moment; "Repeat" replays the current line if you want to hear it again before speaking.

Once a scene's lines are all timed, it shows "Scene complete" and a "Done" button takes you back
out. Teach also offers "Run this and onward" from its start screen, to prepare this scene and
continue straight into the next one without stopping.

## Progress is remembered

Both modes remember where you left off (gated by the "Remember progress" setting): Teach via the
scene's last position in its own line order, Fill-in-Teach via which cluster of context-plus-line
you were on. Leaving mid-way through and coming back later picks up from there rather than
starting over.

## A faster alternative

If timing every line individually feels slow, Fast Path lets you read a short sample of your
lines aloud once and estimates timers for the rest of the scene from your reading pace — see
[Fast Path — timing a whole scene from one short read](fast-path-timing.md).
