title: Entrances and exits
summary: Stage directions mark where your character enters and leaves, with arrows and shading you can see at a glance.

Stage directions can say when a character enters or exits. For your own character, those
directions get a direction arrow and a shaded paper edge, so your arrivals and departures stand
out while you run the scene.

## How it looks

- **Entrance:** a forward arrow (→), with the paper shaded toward the right.
- **Exit:** a back arrow (←), with the paper shaded toward the left.
- The arrows and shading show only for directions that involve your character.
- Turn stage directions off with the "Stage Directions" checkbox in Practice (or "Hide stage
  directions" in Edit). That hides them, and also hides breaks, sound cues and lighting cues.

## Jumping to your next entrance or exit

In Practice, open the ⋮ menu and tap **My Next…**. It offers forward jumps to your next line,
entrance or exit. Choosing an entrance or exit moves you forward to the next one.

- The jump only goes forward. It does not wrap round to the top of the scene.
- If there is no later entrance or exit, a boundary toast says so.

## Setting an entrance or exit

Stage directions are set in Edit mode.

1. Tap **Stage** to add a direction, or tap an existing direction to edit it.
2. Choose the **Direction type**: None, Entrance or Exit.
3. Select the characters who are entering or exiting.

> **Tip:** When you import a script, common entrance and exit wording such as "Enter X" or
> "Exit Y" is picked up automatically. Known cast members are linked to those directions, so
> you rarely need to set them by hand. Check the imported directions in Edit, though: the
> detection works on common wording only.

See also [Cue-to-cue mode](cue-to-cue.md) and [Switching between Practice and Edit](practice-edit-switch.md).
